Title: Security Deposits and Return of Deposit (India - general tenancy principles)

A security deposit (also called an advance or caution deposit) is money paid by a
tenant to a landlord at the start of a tenancy as security against damage to the
property or unpaid rent. Rules vary by state under each state's Rent Control Act
or the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 (where adopted), but common principles are:

- The deposit amount is agreed between landlord and tenant, often capped by state
  law or the rent agreement (many states/model tenancy frameworks suggest a cap of
  around 2 months' rent for unfurnished properties and up to about 6 months for
  furnished properties, though local law and the specific agreement control).
- The landlord must return the deposit within a reasonable period after the tenant
  vacates, after deducting reasonable, documented costs for unpaid rent or damage
  beyond normal wear and tear. Many tenancy agreements or state frameworks specify
  a return period (commonly 30 to 90 days).
- Normal wear and tear (fading paint, minor scuffs) generally cannot be deducted
  from the deposit; the landlord should provide an itemized account of any
  deductions.
- If a landlord unreasonably withholds a deposit, a tenant can send a written
  demand letter, approach the Rent Authority / Rent Court set up under the
  applicable state Rent Act or the Model Tenancy Act (where in force), or pursue
  the matter in the appropriate civil/consumer forum.

This is general information; the exact cap, return period, and forum depend on
the specific state's tenancy law and the terms of the signed rental agreement.
