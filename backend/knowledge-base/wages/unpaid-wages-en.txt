Title: Unpaid Wages - Payment of Wages Act, 1936 (India, general overview)

The Payment of Wages Act, 1936 regulates the timely payment of wages to certain
classes of employed persons in India. Key general principles:

- Wages must be paid within a prescribed period after the wage period ends
  (commonly by the 7th of the following month for establishments with fewer than
  1000 employees, and by the 10th for larger establishments, though this can vary).
- Unauthorized deductions from wages are restricted to specific categories defined
  in the Act (e.g. fines, absence from duty, damage to goods entrusted, specific
  advances) and must follow prescribed limits and procedures.
- If wages are not paid, an employee (or a group of employees, or a registered
  trade union on their behalf) can file a claim before the Authority appointed
  under the Payment of Wages Act, typically within the state Labour Department,
  for recovery of the unpaid amount, and in some cases compensation.
- Employees can also send a written demand to the employer, keep records of hours
  worked and any pay slips, and approach the local Labour Commissioner's office
  for guidance before filing a formal claim.

This is general information; the exact filing authority, limitation period, and
compensation depend on the applicable state rules and the employee's specific
employment category.
