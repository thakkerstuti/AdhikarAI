Title: Consumer Complaints - Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (India, general overview)

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 gives consumers a right to seek redress for
defective goods, deficient services, unfair trade practices, and overcharging.
Key general principles:

- A consumer can file a complaint with the appropriate Consumer Disputes
  Redressal Commission: District Commission for claims up to a prescribed value,
  State Commission for higher-value claims, and the National Commission for the
  highest-value claims and appeals (value thresholds are periodically revised).
- Complaints can also be registered on the National Consumer Helpline (NCH,
  toll-free 1915) or its associated portal (INGRAM/e-daakhil) as a first step,
  which often results in mediation with the seller before a formal case is filed.
- Before filing, a consumer should send a written notice to the seller/service
  provider describing the issue and the resolution sought, and should keep proof
  of purchase, communication records, and evidence of the defect or deficiency.
- Remedies available through the Commissions can include replacement, refund,
  compensation for loss or injury, and removal of the defect or deficiency.

This is general information; the correct forum depends on the claim value and
the nature of the goods or service involved.
